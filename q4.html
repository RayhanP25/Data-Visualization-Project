How have road crash hospitalisation rates changed among younger and older Australians between 2011 and 2021?

About this view

Each cell shows the road crash hospitalisation rate per 100,000 people for an age group in a given year, or the raw number of hospitalisations when that switch is on. Darker cells mean higher rates, using one colour scale across all age groups. Turn on "Scale colours per age group" to see the year-to-year pattern within each row.

Colours are scaled within each age group, so a darker cell means a higher rate than that group's other years. Compare colours along a row, not across rows. Turn the switch off to compare all groups on one scale.

Each line shows the road crash hospitalisation rate per 100,000 people for one age group from 2011 to 2021, or the raw number of hospitalisations when that switch is on. Hover over a point to see its exact rate. Click a legend item to hide its line.

Key findings

  • 17–25 has the highest rate every year.
  • In 2020, the 75+ rate fell 23%, the biggest drop.
  • By 2021, 65–74 and 75+ were still below 2019.
  • 2011 to 2021: 40–64 rose 11%, 0–7 fell 10%.
