About this view

Key Findings

From 2011 to 2021, pedal cyclists experienced the largest increase in hospitalisations, rising by 2,772 cases (51.42%). Car drivers recorded the second-largest increase, with 2,379 additional cases (21.73%). In contrast, pedestrians recorded the largest decrease, with 420 fewer hospitalisations (15.25%).

Methodology

Hospitalisation counts for each road user group in 2011 and 2021 were compared to identify the largest increases and decreases. Absolute change was calculated by subtracting the 2011 count from the 2021 count. Percentage change was calculated by dividing the absolute change by the 2011 count and multiplying by 100. The results were then sorted by absolute change to compare the differences across road user groups.

Data Source

The data was obtained from the Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE), using the Hospitalised Injury Data covering the period from 2011 to 2021. The analysis uses hospitalisation counts categorised by road user group. These figures represent total hospitalisations and do not account for differences in population size or road user exposure.
